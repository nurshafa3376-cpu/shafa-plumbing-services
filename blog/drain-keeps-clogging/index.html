Why Does My Drain Keep Clogging? (And How to Stop It for Good)

By Shafa Plumbing Services · November 15, 2026 · Serving Toronto & the GTA

You clear the clog, the water runs fine... and two weeks later the sink is standing full again. A drain that keeps clogging is not bad luck — it's a message. Something inside the pipe is catching everything that passes, and until that something is dealt with, the clog will keep coming back.

Here is what is usually going on, what actually fixes it, and the habits that stop it for good.

The usual causes of a drain that keeps clogging

  • Grease and cooking oil (kitchen sinks). Grease goes down liquid and cools into a sticky coating on the pipe wall. Every meal adds another layer, and food bits stick to it. This is the most common cause of repeat kitchen clogs.
  • Hair and soap scum (bathroom sinks, tubs, showers). Hair tangles into a net, soap scum glues it together, and the net catches everything after it.
  • "Flushable" wipes. Most do not break down like toilet paper. They snag on any rough spot in the line and build a dam. Baby wipes, cleaning wipes and paper towels do the same.
  • Food scraps and coffee grounds. Coffee grounds clump like wet sand. Rice and pasta swell with water. A garburator helps, but it doesn't make a drain invincible.
  • Scale and rust in older pipes. Many Toronto and Scarborough homes have older lines that have narrowed inside over decades — a pipe that was 2 inches wide may have half that room left, so it clogs on things a healthy pipe would pass.
  • Tree roots in the main sewer line. In older neighbourhoods with clay sewer pipes, roots find the joints and grow inside the line. If several drains in the house are slow at once, this is a prime suspect.

Why the clog keeps coming back

Most quick fixes only punch a small hole through the blockage. The water runs again — through a tunnel in the middle of the grease, hair or roots that are still there. The tunnel is narrow, it catches new debris fast, and the drain clogs again within weeks.

That is the difference between clearing a drain and cleaning it. Clearing opens a hole. Cleaning removes the buildup from the pipe wall, or finds and fixes the real cause — a root mass, a sagging section of pipe, or a line that has narrowed with age. If your drain clogs more than twice in a few months, the pipe itself needs attention, not another bottle of cleaner.

What NOT to pour down a clogged drain

Chemical drain cleaners, used again and again, generate heat and harsh fumes, can soften or damage older pipe and seals, and rarely touch the real buildup. They also sit in the clog as a caustic puddle — which is dangerous for whoever opens that pipe next.
  • Boiling water by the kettle-full into a fully blocked sink. It has nowhere to go, and on some plastic fittings the shock of heat does more harm than good. Warm-to-hot tap water is the safer version of this idea.
  • Anything that isn't toilet paper into the toilet. Not wipes (even "flushable" ones), not paper towel, not cotton pads, not dental floss.

Safe things you can try first

  1. Plunge properly. For a sink, block the overflow hole with a wet cloth, add enough water to cover the plunger cup, and plunge with strong, steady strokes for 20–30 seconds.
  2. Hot tap water + a little dish soap for a slow kitchen sink — the soap helps loosen fresh grease. Good as weekly maintenance too.
  3. Clean the pop-up stopper in bathroom sinks. Unscrew or lift it out — the hair net hanging off it is often the whole problem.
  4. Use drain strainers everywhere — kitchen sink, tub and shower. Catching hair and food before they enter the pipe is the simplest prevention there is.
  5. A basic hand snake can pull a near-surface hair clog from a tub or sink. Work gently — forcing a snake can damage older pipe.

If none of that restores a full, fast drain, the blockage is deeper than DIY tools reach.

Habits that stop clogs for good

  • Grease goes in a can, not the sink. Let it cool, pour it into a container, and put it in the garbage.
  • Scrape plates into the bin before rinsing, and run cold water when using the garburator so grease stays solid and gets carried through.
  • Only toilet paper goes in the toilet. Everything else goes in the trash, no exceptions.
  • Run hot tap water for 30 seconds after washing greasy pans.
  • Keep the strainers in. Most repeat clogs start with what should have been caught at the surface.

When it's time to call a plumber

Call a professional when:

  • The same drain clogs again within a few weeks of clearing it
  • More than one drain in the house is slow at the same time (a main-line warning sign)
  • You hear gurgling from drains or the toilet when water runs elsewhere
  • There is a sewer smell from drains, or water backs up into a tub, shower or floor drain
  • A plunger and a hand snake don't restore full flow

These signs point past the trap under the sink — to buildup along the line, roots, or a problem in the main sewer. A camera inspection shows exactly what is inside the pipe and where, so the fix targets the cause instead of guessing. That is how a repeat clog actually ends.
