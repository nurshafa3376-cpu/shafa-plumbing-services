Kitec Plumbing in Toronto Condos: What It Is and When to Replace It

By Shafa Plumbing Services · November 17, 2026 · Serving Toronto & the GTA

If your Toronto condo was built between the mid-1990s and the late 2000s, there is a fair chance the water pipes inside your walls are Kitec. Owners usually first hear the name from a home inspector, an insurance company or a condo status certificate — often without a clear explanation of what it is or how urgent it is.

This article explains what Kitec is, why it fails, how to check whether your unit has it, and how to decide between watching it and replacing it.

What is Kitec plumbing?

Kitec is a plumbing system made by the Canadian company IPEX and sold in Canada from about 1995 to 2007. It was popular with builders because it was cheaper and quicker to install than copper. You will find it in houses too, but Toronto's condo boom of those years means a large share of downtown towers were plumbed with it.

A Kitec system has two parts:

  • The pipes — flexible plastic pipes, usually bright blue for cold water and orange for hot water, stamped with names like "Kitec" or "XPA."
  • The fittings — brass connectors that join the pipes to each other and to your fixtures.

The pipes themselves have held up reasonably in many buildings. The fittings are where the trouble starts.

Why Kitec fails

The brass used in many Kitec fittings contains a high amount of zinc. In certain water conditions, that zinc slowly leaches out of the brass — a process called dezincification. As it happens, the fitting becomes weak and porous, and a crusty white or green build-up can form on it and narrow the water passage inside.

The result is a fitting that can start to weep, leak, or in the worst case burst. Inside a condo wall, that does not just damage your unit — water runs down into the units below, which is why condo corporations and insurers take Kitec seriously.

Kitec was the subject of class-action lawsuits in Canada and the United States, and a settlement fund was set up in 2011. That claim deadline has passed, so the cost of dealing with Kitec now falls on the homeowner — one more reason to deal with it on your own schedule instead of after a failure.

How to check if your condo has Kitec

You do not need to open any walls to get a good first answer. Check the exposed plumbing:

  1. Look near your water heater or under your kitchen and bathroom sinks. Kitec pipes are usually blue and orange and are printed with "Kitec," "XPA," or similar markings along their length.
  2. Look at the fittings. Brass fittings with white or green crusty deposits, or fittings that look swollen or corroded, are a warning sign.
  3. Check your condo documents. Status certificates and building engineering reports often state whether the building was plumbed with Kitec.
  4. Note the building's age. A building finished between about 1995 and 2007 is in the Kitec window. Buildings outside those years rarely have it.

If you find orange and blue pipes but no markings, a plumber can usually confirm what you have in a short visit.

The insurance and resale problem

Even before anything leaks, Kitec can cost you:

  • Insurance. Some insurers charge higher premiums for homes with Kitec, add conditions, or decline to cover water damage from it. Others ask for proof that it has been replaced.
  • Selling your unit. Kitec must be disclosed to buyers. Some buyers walk away; others reduce their offer by roughly the cost of replacement. A unit with the plumbing already replaced is simply easier to sell.

None of this means a condo with Kitec is worthless — thousands of Toronto owners live with it. But it does affect the value and insurability of your home, not just your plumbing.

Replace it now, or watch and wait?

There is no single right answer, but these factors point most owners in the right direction:

Replacement should move up your list if:

  • Fittings already show white or green corrosion, dampness, or slow drips
  • Your insurer is raising your premium or asking about Kitec
  • You plan to sell in the next few years
  • Your building has already had Kitec failures in other units
  • The system is simply at the age where failures become common — most of it is now 20 to 30 years old

Monitoring may be reasonable if the fittings look clean and dry, you are not selling, and your insurer is satisfied — provided you actually look at the exposed fittings a couple of times a year and know where your unit's main water shut-off valve is. The owners who get hurt are usually the ones who never looked at all.

One honest caution: replacing only the worst-looking fittings is not a real fix. Every fitting in the system is the same age and the same material. A full replacement of the water lines is the only permanent answer.

What replacing Kitec involves

In a condo unit, replacement usually means removing the Kitec water lines and installing modern PEX or copper piping in their place. The work is normally done unit by unit, and it requires booking a water shut-down with your condo management, since the building's water has to be off while lines are disconnected and reconnected.

A proper job starts with an assessment: how much Kitec your unit has, where it runs, and what access is needed. Small openings in drywall are sometimes required to reach pipes; ask any plumber you hire to state in the written quote exactly what is included and what finishing work, if any, is left for you. Keep the replacement paperwork — it is the proof your insurer and future buyers will want to see.
