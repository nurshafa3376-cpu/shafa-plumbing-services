Frozen Pipes in Toronto: What to Do Before They Burst

By Shafa Plumbing Services · November 15, 2026 · Serving Toronto & the GTA

Every Toronto winter, the same thing happens: a cold snap hits overnight, and by morning a pipe has frozen — or worse, burst and flooded a basement. Frozen pipes are one of the most common winter plumbing emergencies in the GTA, and most of the damage is preventable if you know the warning signs and act early.

This guide covers why pipes freeze in Toronto homes, how to spot a frozen pipe, how to thaw one safely, and the prevention steps that actually work.

Why pipes freeze in Toronto homes

When water inside a pipe freezes, it expands. That expansion builds enormous pressure between the ice and your faucet, and the pipe can split — often not at the frozen spot itself, but somewhere along the line. The pipes most at risk are:

  • Pipes in unheated areas: basements, crawl spaces, garages and attics
  • Pipes running along exterior walls, especially older Toronto homes with little insulation
  • Outdoor taps and garden hose connections left connected over winter
  • Pipes in vacant or lightly heated homes and units

The danger zone is any stretch of cold weather well below freezing — the kind of multi-day deep freeze Toronto and the GTA get every winter.

Warning signs a pipe is frozen

  • Little or no water coming from a faucet on a cold morning
  • Frost on the outside of an exposed pipe
  • Strange smells from a drain or faucet (ice is blocking the line)
  • Unusual sounds — banging or whistling when you turn a tap on
  • Bulging or cracked sections of pipe — this is an emergency sign

If a pipe is frozen: safe steps

  1. Keep the faucet open. As the ice melts, water needs somewhere to go — an open tap relieves pressure.
  2. Apply gentle heat to the frozen section: a hair dryer, a heating pad, or towels soaked in hot water all work. Start near the faucet and work back toward the coldest section.
  3. Raise the heat in that part of the home and open cabinet doors under sinks so warm air reaches the pipes.
  4. Check for leaks as it thaws. A pipe that split while frozen often only starts leaking once the ice melts.
Never use a blowtorch, propane heater or any open flame on a frozen pipe — it can boil the water inside, burst the pipe, and start a fire. Never pour boiling water directly on a frozen pipe either; the shock can crack it.

If a pipe bursts

  1. Shut off your main water valve immediately. Every homeowner should know where this is before winter — it is usually where the water line enters the home, near the meter in the basement.
  2. Open your faucets to drain the lines and relieve pressure.
  3. Turn off electricity to any flooded area if water is near outlets or appliances, and only if you can do it safely and stay dry.
  4. Call a plumber. A burst pipe is an emergency — the faster the water is controlled, the less damage to floors, walls and belongings.

How to prevent frozen pipes this winter

  • Insulate exposed pipes in the basement, crawl space and garage with foam pipe sleeves — cheap and very effective.
  • Disconnect garden hoses and shut off and drain outdoor taps before the first hard freeze.
  • Keep the heat on — never let your home drop below about 13°C (55°F), even when you're away.
  • On extreme cold nights, let a faucet drip slowly. Moving water is much harder to freeze, and a slow drip relieves pressure.
  • Open cabinet doors under kitchen and bathroom sinks on very cold nights so warm air circulates around the pipes.
  • Seal drafts where cold air hits pipes — around foundation cracks, rim joists and pipe entry points.
  • Know your main shut-off valve and make sure it works. If it hasn't been touched in years, have it checked before you need it in an emergency.

When to call a plumber

Call a professional if you can't find the frozen section, the pipe is inside a wall, you see bulging or cracks, a thaw attempt isn't restoring water, or a pipe has already burst.
